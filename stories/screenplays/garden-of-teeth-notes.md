# "The Garden of Teeth": notes before the screenplay (Claude Code, 2026-10-03)

## The brief (after Thorns and Static)
- A new film, very similar to the last one: the goth look, the Halloween dressing, the orange and black.
- **Fix 1:** the same woman in every shot, with a plain pale goth face.
  - Cause found in Thorns and Static: "two small pointed vampire fangs" in her description was drawn as points
    hanging over the lower lip at the mouth corners (snake-bite piercings), plus a drip at the chin.
  - Shots without `[cast]` (5, 9, 13, 16) drew other women.
- **Fix 2:** a continued story that reads as a real narrative, not random things stitched together. The ask: make the scenes tie together so a viewer clearly sees one narrative, as a director would.
- **Fix 3:** scarier.
- **More of what worked:**
  - the round scary things (a frame picked from the last film: the giant black rose head with a ring of teeth, shot 8);
  - the wall of water full of glowing pumpkins behind her (shot 22, the redo).

## How a viewer sees a narrative (the director's tools used here)
1. **A goal in the first shot:** she must cross the garden to get home, to the lighthouse.
2. **Rules said out loud in the hook, then broken one by one.** That is the fairy-tale engine: an interdiction, its
   violation, and the consequence:
   - "Stay on the path. Don't let the pumpkins see you. And never wake the rose."
   - The viewer knows the rules, so each break reads as cause and effect.
3. **A motivated break:** the garden calls in her mother's voice, so she steps off the path. That is horror (the
   mimic) and it is clear (her real mother is at the lighthouse, and she looks there first).
4. **One antagonist that persists:** the Rose (the round scary thing) is the same creature every time. It sleeps
   (1, 2), wakes (7), hunts (8-10), follows her underground through its roots (16), looms at the climax (18-19) and
   dies in the flood (21). The pumpkins are its eyes: they light up when they see her, in the garden and in the rooms.
5. **A visible destination and constant geography.**
   - The garden lies in a hollow below a stone sea wall; the sea on the far side is higher than the garden.
   - The lighthouse stands at the end of the wall.
   - Shot 2 shows all of it, so the climax needs no explanation.
6. **A plan the audience can follow.**
   - "If it ever woke, I run to the sea" (8).
   - The salt water seeping into the rooms points the way (15).
   - At the floodgate she says why: "the garden feared only one thing. The sea" (19).
7. **She causes the climax:** she opens the gate, and the sea drowns the garden, the pumpkins and the Rose. The
   wave catches her too.
8. **Time moves forward:** sunset, then dusk with a storm, then the rooms, then the last light at the wall, then dawn.
   The sky tells the viewer where they are in the story.
9. **A resolution and a scare:** at dawn she wakes alive with home in sight, and a tiny black rose bud opens on a ring
   of teeth beside her hand.

## Candidate beats (kept *)
1. * Hook: the rules spoken at the gate with the sleeping Rose looming behind her, its petals breathing on teeth.
2. Hook alt: the Rose's maw snapping at the lens as a flash-forward (cut: breaks the chronology the brief asked for).
3. * Aerial: the hollow, the sea wall with the sea higher than the garden, the lighthouse, the path, the sleeping Rose.
4. * She walks the path, eyes front, past dark unlit pumpkins as tall as she is.
5. * Her mother's voice from the flowers: "Leave the path. Come to Mother."
6. * Close-up: she looks at the flowers, then at the far lighthouse (where Mother really is), then back.
7. * She steps off the path, and every pumpkin lights at once and turns to her.
8. * The Rose wakes: petals peel open on a ring of ivory teeth; the pumpkins' flames lean toward her.
9. * Hiding: "It's awake. Mother said if it ever woke, I run to the sea."
10. * The hunt: round toothed rose heads lunge from both sides of the path.
11. * The vines drag her toward the teeth; she tears free.
12. * The ground opens; she drops.
13. * The fall past roots and carved pumpkins wedged in the shaft walls.
14. * Under the garden: the gothic backrooms, its roots through the ceiling.
15. * The rooms watch: a carved pumpkin in every room lights up as she passes.
16. A mirror in every room shows the Rose instead of her (the video model would draw her; cut).
17. * Salt water seeping across the carpet: "The sea is right above me."
18. * The dark follows: lights die room by room, roots pour along the ceiling after her.
19. * The door bursts open onto the storm at the foot of the sea wall.
20. * The sea wall: floodgate, wheel, lighthouse above, the Rose rising over the hollow, pumpkins rolling at her.
21. * At the wheel: "Mother always said the garden feared only one thing. The sea."
22. * The gate bursts; the sea comes in.
23. * The flood drowns the garden; the Rose thrashes and is torn apart.
24. * The wave catches her (the favourite frame from the last film, now with her face).
25. * Dawn: the drowned garden, the Rose's torn head on the sand; she wakes; her mother's lantern at the lighthouse door.
26. * The button: a black bud opens on tiny teeth beside her hand.
27. Mother appears and hugs her (a second face to keep consistent, and it softens the scare; cut).
