# "Thorns and Static": notes before the screenplay (Claude Code, 2026-10-03)

## The brief, every concrete thing (each must appear)
- A TikTok in 9:16 vertical, about three minutes, the first six seconds very engaging.
- Halloween and goth: everything dark and black with orange; an orange sunset; very dramatic; realistic.
- A girl who is a vampire: a very pale Slavic face, long black hair, Victorian goth attire.
- She wanders a forest of giant flowers towering over her. The flowers are alive, with thorns, and want to kill her
  and eat her. Thorned vines are alive and try to capture her.
- Evil pumpkins with glowing eyes, quite scary.
- Lightning strikes, very realistic.
- She runs to escape. She falls into a hole and lands in the backrooms, out of the forest: a terrifying maze where
  everything repeats, all gothic.
- Clean and engaging, like a fairy tale with two worlds.
- A very realistic tsunami: she runs from it, it catches up, sweeps everything up (all the pumpkins), covers her;
  she runs, falls; then static.

## The want (drama), in one sentence
A vampire who has always been the monster is now prey to everything in a fairy-tale world (flowers, vines,
pumpkins, the maze, the sea), and the one thing she wants after two hundred years in the dark is the thing that
should kill her: the sun. Every shot plays it once; no shot explains it.

## Fairy-tale bones (never named in the film)
Thumbelina and Alice's Garden of Live Flowers (giant flowers), Alice's fall down the hole into a hall of doors (the
backrooms), the Little Mermaid's ending (the sea takes her at sunset). The two worlds: the organic garden at sunset,
and the artificial endless rooms. The sea is where they meet and where both are swept away.

## Candidate beats (brainstorm; kept ones marked *)
1. * Hook: she sprints at the lens, a giant black rose head snaps shut just behind her hair, lightning.
2. Hook alt: extreme close-up, her eyes snap open among black petals, a flower head opening above. (slower; cut)
3. Hook alt: lightning hits a jack-o'-lantern in the first frame. (kept as its own shot later)
4. * Aerial dive over the forest: flowers thirty metres tall, a ruined chapel spire, a castle far off, ravens.
5. * Hiding, back to a thorned stem: "In my day, flowers had the decency to wait until you were dead."
6. Line: "I have frightened kings. Nobody has ever carved me a face." (weak, cut)
7. * Pumpkins taller than a man ignite one by one along a wrought-iron fence, eyes all turned on her.
8. Line to a pumpkin: "Stop staring. I'm the monster here." (explains the conceit; cut)
9. "Two hundred years of being the monster, and I never once asked how it felt." (explains; cut, and the
   "two hundred years" is saved for the beach)
10. * Thorned vines slither across the petals, coil her ankle; she tears free, lace rips.
11. * Lightning strikes a giant jack-o'-lantern; it bursts; the flash lights the whole forest.
12. * Cornered at a broken stone angel, a flower head bends over her: "Go on, then. I've been bitten before.
    That's how all of this started."
13. * The flower lunges, crushes the angel's wing where she stood; she is already running.
14. A thorn cuts her palm; she looks at it: "Rude. I was saving that." (funny, breaks the dread; cut)
15. * The petal ground caves in; she drops out of frame; petals sucked after her.
16. * The fall, from below: a shaft of roots, candles in niches, old televisions in the earth hissing static. Sets up
    the ending.
17. * Landing in the gothic backrooms: black damask, faded orange pattern, amber panels buzzing, damp red carpet.
18. * Close-up: "No windows. No sun. No end. I used to pray for a place like this."
19. * Walking: the same red velvet armchair and the same television, room after room.
20. * Beside the television: "That's the eleventh time I've passed this television, and there's still nothing
    on." (the set-up for the static button)
21. A mirror in every room that never shows her. (the video model would draw the reflection; cut)
22. A portrait of her in every room, painted in 1842. (needs her face in a painting; cut)
23. * Far down the corridor, a girl exactly like her, back turned, begins to turn her head.
24. * The lights die room by room behind her, the dark racing at her; she runs.
25. * A door with orange light under it; surf behind it; sunlight floods her face.
26. * A lone door frame on a black-sand beach at sunset; the garden's flowers and pumpkins line the dunes.
27. * Knees-up at the water: "Two hundred years in the dark. Nobody ever told me the sun was warm."
28. * The sea drains away (a real tsunami's drawback): bared seabed, flapping fish, boats on their sides; the
    pumpkins' flames gutter.
29. * The tsunami on the horizon, crest orange in the sunset, lightning behind; she runs.
30. * The surge bursts through the dunes, snaps the giant stems, lifts and tumbles the glowing pumpkins.
31. * She falls; white water crashes over her and fills the frame; dark, bubbles.
32. * Static fills the frame: "nothing on".
33. A narrator's "Once upon a time" over the aerial. (cut: the girl would mouth it, and it explains)

## Order and escalation
Garden (flowers, pumpkins, vines, lightning, the lunge) -> the fall -> the rooms (sameness, the TV, herself, the
dark) -> the door -> the sun (her one moment of grace) -> the sea leaves -> the wave -> static.
Her voice escalates too: dry wit (5, 12), wonder curdling (18), brittle (20), undone (27).

## The rig's rules applied
- One speaker in the whole film (Zorya), five lines, each 13-15 words in an 8 s shot, said standing or kneeling
  still, frontal or three-quarter, nothing at the mouth, no smile.
- Speaking framings vary (the brief asked for more than chin-and-face): medium close-up (3), knees up (7), close-up
  (12), waist up (14), knees up (19). Interim rules 6b867dd sign off close-up to full length.
- `[cast=zorya]` only where she is medium or closer (it composes at medium scale); the wide and action shots use
  `[still]` and her description, so the image model keeps their scale.
- `[quality]` on the four spectacle shots: the hook (1), the lightning strike (6), the wave rising (21), the surge (22).
- Stress cues are written without quote marks ("leaning on the word bitten"), so the only quoted span is the line.
