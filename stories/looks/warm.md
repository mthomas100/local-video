# Warm — the in-between places (no look test; built from six reference photos, not included)

The six reference photos ARE the look: a yellow escalator atrium, a bluebonnet corridor, a
rainbow ball pit, a pastel stairwell, a white-tiled spiral pool, a pink-lit flooded underpass.
The family of clowns wanders them as one quiet, patient hunt, following a trail of objects
someone just left behind. Each space keeps its own palette and light straight from its image;
the bible holds style only, never the palettes (they must not tint every shot).

## The eight slots (shootable words)

1. Palette: per space, never global — warm yellow atrium light, white columns and blue
   flowers, saturated ball-pit primaries, pastel pink-and-mint stripes, white grid tile and
   teal water, white tile with pink fluorescent. One signature accent: the red balloon.
2. Light: motivated by each place itself — warm atrium skylight, sun through corridor
   windows, flat ceiling fluorescents in the ball pit, soft stairwell daylight, cool light
   on the pool water, pink fluorescent tubes over the flooded underpass.
3. Camera behaviour: gentle, unhurried — long locked-off wides for the empty spaces, slow
   push-ins and steady tracking for the faces. Never handheld jitter.
4. Lens feel: shallow depth of field, background soft; "ordinary 40mm lens, a clean
   rectangular full-frame image". Wides keep the whole frame in focus.
5. Production design and wardrobe: the six liminal spaces exactly as photographed; a family
   of four real adult clowns (white face paint, red noses, tattered costumes with silver
   bells), the child holding a real red balloon that grows each space. No crowds, no extras.
6. Weather and time: interior, timeless; each space lit by its own light, no day or night.
7. Pacing: slow and quiet — one steady shot per beat, medium and close for the faces, long
   wides for the empty rooms. The dialogue is polite, chatty, almost sweet, and that's what
   makes it wrong.
8. Sound and score: a low fluorescent hum, room tone, the soft squeak of oversized shoes
   somewhere off-screen; laughter only ever from the clowns, always at the wrong moment.

## Style sentence (append at the very END of every scene; style only, no story, no grain, no lens/format words)

Photorealistic live-action, a quiet uncanny dread, gentle steady camera, shallow depth of
field, an ordinary 40mm lens, a clean rectangular full-frame image, real adult actors who
resemble no celebrity.

## Sound and score line (the film's SOUND= ambience)

A low fluorescent hum, a distant room tone, the soft squeak of oversized shoes somewhere
off-screen.

## Wardrobe palette per character

- Mopsy: white face paint, a round red nose, a bright orange curly wig, a patched
  mustard-and-rust costume with silver bells.
- Reginald: white face paint with a painted-on too-wide red smile, a round red nose, a neat
  grey wig, a burgundy-and-gold costume with a silver bell collar.
- Tulip (child): white face paint with a small red nose, a cream costume with silver bells,
  holding a real red balloon.
- Agnes (eldest): white face paint with a cracked porcelain finish, a small red nose, a long
  silver wig, a lavender costume with silver bells.

## Notes

The six places are real, from reference photos (not included): each establishing shot opens on the
actual photo via `[place=...]` and the clowns walk into it. The look is the liminal uncanny —
warm and familiar, then wrong. The red balloon is the only accent and the film's clock: it
grows each space and no one comments on it. The clowns are real adult actors in makeup, no
celebrity likeness. The dialogue is polite and chatty; one visible speaker per speaking shot,
facing the camera, face lit. The family stands too still only in wide shots without a line,
never in a speaking shot.
