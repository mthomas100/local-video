# Cinematic vocabulary that this model actually follows

Use plain film-set language. Pick one item per slot; more than one camera move per shot
reads as jitter.

## Camera moves (with speed and landing point)
slow push-in ending close on X · slow pull-back revealing X · tracking alongside X at walking
pace · low tracking shot at knee height · slow orbit around X at shoulder height · half-orbit
that ends on her face · handheld follow behind X · static locked-off frame · slow pan left to
right across X · tilt up from the boots to the face · crane up over the rooftops · overhead
top-down view descending slowly · over-the-shoulder on X looking at Y · rack focus from the
glass in front to her eyes behind · whip pan to X (use once, if ever).

## Shot sizes
extreme close-up on the eyes / hands · close-up on the face · medium close-up · medium
two-shot · medium shot from the waist · wide establishing shot (for 2-3 s, then cut in) ·
insert shot of the object.

## Light (source, direction, colour)
one window on the left, cold blue morning · warm practical lamp behind them · a single sodium
streetlight overhead · candlelight from below · flat overcast light, no shadows · low golden
hour backlight, rim on the hair · neon from the shop sign, magenta on one cheek · headlights
sweeping across the wall · firelight flickering on the faces · moonlight through blinds.

## Atmosphere (motion the model loves)
drifting frost · dust in a shaft of light · steam from a samovar · falling snow · rain on the
glass · sea spray · mist over the water · sparks · smoke curling · pigeons taking off · wind in
her hair and in the wheat.

## Action verbs that read well
walks, turns, kneels, reaches, hands, sets down, looks up, leans in, steps back, laughs,
exhales, wipes, pours, opens, closes, lifts, lowers. Avoid juggling, jumping, fast spins,
fights: non-linear fast motion smears.

## Emotion as physical cue
boredom: a slow blink, a flick of the hand, looking past him · surprise: eyes widen, a half
step back · tenderness: a hand that hesitates then lands · grief: a swallow, a held breath ·
joy: a laugh that starts before she means it · resolve: the jaw sets, she stands.

## Sound (always the last sentence)
ambience (wind on ice, tram bells, a samovar hiss, surf, a kitchen clock) → one specific sound
(a spoon on glass, boots on snow, a crystal chime) → a voice (a short quoted line, a laugh, a
gasp). Music: "a gentle swelling orchestral note", "a slow accordion waltz". Volume words
work: whisper, calls, shouts.

## Dialogue syntax (2026-09-27: lip sync measured with rig/audit/lipsync.py)
The spoken line is the shot's ACTION, right after who is in the frame, never after the ambience:
`Mia looks at her father and says, in a small tired voice: 'Are we still in the same building?'` One visible speaker,
facing the camera or three-quarter, about 2 words a second (10-16 words in an 8 s shot), and no grin, smile or laugh
in that shot: lines appended after the sound, 2-4 words long, on grinning faces had the mouth 0.4 s ahead of the voice. Say the language or
accent if it matters ("in Russian"). Songs: "she hums a slow lullaby".

## Style sentence (goes last; safe wording)
"Real documentary-style live-action footage, shot on a digital cinema camera with an ordinary
40mm lens, a clean rectangular full-frame image, shallow depth of field, natural light, natural
skin texture, real adult actors who resemble no celebrity, subtle restrained performances."
For animation on purpose: "hand-drawn 2D animation with painted backgrounds" or "stop-motion
puppets with visible felt texture". For period: name the year and three period objects.
