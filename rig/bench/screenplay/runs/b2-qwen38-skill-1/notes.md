# NOTES — "The Apes of South of Market" (working)

## Premise
A hushed, reverent British nature-documentary narrator films a San Francisco tech startup during Halloween week
as if the office were a wildlife warren: the herd, the nests, the great migration to the snack kitchen, the alpha
engineer, the courtship rituals of the costume contest. The employees do interview-to-camera confessions, stone
serious about absurd things. Each Halloween the grid fails; during the blackout the twist lands: the narrator is a
raccoon living in the ceiling above the snack kitchen — the one predator the herd has never noticed.

## The game (repeats, escalates)
The contrast: whispering David-Attenborough-style reverence applied to open-plan office trivia, while the
employees' dead-serious interviews top the narrator's animal framing with corporate absurdity. Every shot:
narrator says something reverent about an animal behaviour; the cut to interview or background action tops it.
Escalation: daily rituals → night display (costume contest) → crisis (the October grid failure) → the predator
reveal. A lean grey raccoon with a black mask and ringed tail sits UNNOTICED in the background of every shot —
including behind every speaker — so the twist is rewatchable. Nobody ever notices it; its mouth stays closed
always; the narrator is only ever a voice.

## Shape
120 s = 15 shots × 8 s. Vertical 9:16, 1080×1920 HD. Spine (documentary/mock-doc): habitat → herd → migration →
alpha → courtship display → judging → the matriarch → crisis → survival → the unseen predator → button.
[still]+[quality]: shots 1, 12, 15. Interviews: medium close-up, waist up (measured sync framing). One speaker
per shot, one quoted line per shot, 10–18 words.
