# THE HABITAT

**Premise.** A hushed, reverent BBC-style nature documentary narrates a San Francisco fintech startup during Halloween week as if the open-plan office were a savannah: the 15:15 migration to the kombucha tap, the alpha engineer who never queues, the costume contest as plumage display, the mating call that nobody answers. The employees themselves sit for confessional interviews with grey foam microphones and describe their jobs with the same deadpan gravity as the narrator — total seriousness, zero self-awareness. **The game:** every shot plays the same contrast once — human behaviour described as animal behaviour, played completely straight — and the cutaways and background figures quietly contradict, confirm, or one-up whatever the speaker just said (the dog in the pumpkin costume leads the migration; the empty Series A investor costume glides past a man praising its vest; the whole tribe watches the narrator on television). **The twist:** the narrator's own breathing gives him away — the camera pulls back from under a desk to reveal he is Toby, an unpaid intern in a ghillie suit who cannot afford rent. The office then turns the lens back on him, and the CEO delivers the button: "We wanted a world-class nature documentarian. This one was completely free."

## Shape

- **Aspect:** 9:16 vertical HD (1080x1920)
- **Length:** ~2 minutes 8 seconds
- **Shots:** 16 continuous shots, 8 seconds each

## The Game (spine)

Nature-documentary grammar applied to office life, never explained, always escalating: habitat → migration → waterhole hierarchy → plumage and predators → mating call → nesting → the observer revealed as an animal → the ecosystem observing him back. Every shot either (a) has the whispering narrator label a mundane act as instinct, or (b) has an employee say something solemn while the background quietly outdoes it. No shot explains the joke.

## Cast (fixed descriptions — reuse word for word)

- **NARRATOR (voice-over until shot 13):** a hushed, reverent British voice, the voice of a nature documentary, whispering as though afraid of startling the animals.
- **KEVIN OSEI** — a 38-year-old Ghanaian-British man, black buzz cut, matte black helmet-style headphones worn permanently over his ears, charcoal hoodie, calm immovable face. Alpha engineer.
- **PRIYA RAMAN** — a 29-year-old Indian-American woman, severe centre-parted black bob, round gold-rimmed glasses, grey puffer vest over a black turtleneck. Growth lead.
- **DEREK LU** — a 26-year-old Chinese-American man, thin sandy-blond hair combed across his scalp, small stud earring in his left ear, wrinkled pale blue button-down shirt. Solutions architect.
- **MARISOL REYES** — a 24-year-old Filipina-American woman, dark brown hair in a high tight ponytail, rust-orange flannel shirt, silver hoop earrings. People operations lead.
- **GERALD SHAW** — a 45-year-old white man, neat silver-grey beard, black cashmere V-neck sweater, silver-framed aviator glasses. CEO.
- **TOBY** — a 21-year-old white man, shaggy brown mop of hair, olive-green ghillie suit jacket over a white T-shirt and khaki shorts, black lanyard with a laminated badge. Intern.
- **THE DOG** — a golden retriever wearing an inflated orange pumpkin costume with a green stem collar. Silent, recurring; the film's Greek chorus.

**Standing setting (describe physically in every shot):** a converted-warehouse tech startup office in the South of Market district of San Francisco: exposed steel beams, raw red brick walls, long oak desks on rolling white legs with glowing monitors, orange string lights along the beams, carved jack-o'-lanterns on the desks; a kitchen area with a white tiled wall, three brass kombucha taps on a counter, and a glowing neon pink "SNACKS" sign; an entrance area with a double-leaf glass door onto a pavement and a large carved jack-o'-lantern on a wooden trestle table against brick.

**Interview prop (every interview shot):** a grey foam-covered handheld interview microphone held still at chest height, below the chin, never crossing the jaw line.

**Lip-sync rule observed throughout:** one visible speaker per speaking shot, medium close-up or close-up, facing camera, no lean or step while speaking, no grin, silent background figures only, one line of 12–16 words per 8 seconds. Voice-over shots contain no on-screen speaker at all.

**On-screen text:** kept to two tiny, simple instances — the neon pink 'SNACKS' sign (one word, repeated across kitchen shots, so the model can converge on it) and the cardboard 'DISRUPTION' sign in shot 8 (huge marker strokes, no other writing). No screens, chat windows or documents are ever legible.

## Gags kept (from the brainstorm)

Migration at 15:15 to the waterhole / the alpha never queues and nobody says anything / the pumpkin-costumed dog leads the herd and lies across the queue / the empty Series A investor costume that moves by itself / "my costume is disruption" — in her normal clothes, sign and all / the mating call that the valley returns to its inbox / the vest vintage from a round that did not close / nesting from all the season provides / the narrator caught under a desk in a ghillie suit / the intern nesting in the same nest, the dog at his heel / the tribe huddled around a television watching the observer / the CEO's hiring budget as the final button.

## THE SHOTS

**1. EXTREME WIDE — exterior, SoMa side street, dusk, 8 s — no on-screen speaker (narrator voice-over).**
A vertical extreme wide shot of a four-storey converted red-brick warehouse on a quiet South of Market side street in San Francisco at dusk, tall arched industrial windows behind which warm light and orange string lights are coming on, a bike lane and a single parked scooter on the asphalt in the foreground, the last copper light of the day raking across the brick. The camera drifts slowly forward and upward from a low angle toward the glowing windows. No one speaks on camera. Sound: distant city hum and traffic, wind, and a hushed, reverent British narrator's voice-over, the voice of a nature documentary, whispering: 'Halloween week, in a fintech ecosystem on the edge of San Francisco.'

**2. MEDIUM CLOSE-UP INTERVIEW — open-plan office, 8 s — KEVIN.**
Medium close-up of KEVIN OSEI — a 38-year-old Ghanaian-British man, black buzz cut, matte black helmet-style headphones worn permanently over his ears, charcoal hoodie, calm immovable face — seated squarely facing the camera inside the converted-warehouse office of exposed steel beams, raw red brick walls and long oak desks with glowing monitors, orange string lights along the beams and carved jack-o'-lanterns on the desks; behind him on the right, out of focus, a colleague in a grey hoodie silently stacks flattened cardboard delivery boxes into a small mound. Kevin holds the grey foam-covered interview microphone still at chest height below his chin, eyes level and unblinking, brows raised a fraction, and says: 'I stand. I earned this standing desk in the incident of two thousand twenty-two.' His voice is a low, measured baritone, one small pause between each word, landing flat. His head does not move. The room is dim, lit by monitor glow and warm orange string lights. Sound: quiet keyboard clatter from off-screen, low room tone.

**3. WIDE TRACKING — open-plan office to kitchen, 8 s — no on-screen speaker (narrator voice-over).**
A low vertical full shot gliding steadily alongside a procession of six employees walking in single file from left to right across the open-plan office — exposed steel beams, raw red brick walls, long oak desks with glowing monitors, orange string lights overhead, carved jack-o'-lanterns on the desks — each carrying a labelled ceramic mug, led by THE DOG, a golden retriever wearing an inflated orange pumpkin costume with a green stem collar; they head for the kitchen area with its white tiled wall, three brass kombucha taps and glowing neon pink 'SNACKS' sign. The camera tracks at knee height, smooth as a documentary hide, nobody speaking on camera. Sound: shuffling trainers, ceramic mugs clinking, a tap priming, and the hushed British narrator: 'At fifteen fifteen, the whole tribe rises and migrates together to the waterhole.'

**4. MEDIUM CLOSE-UP INTERVIEW — kitchen, 8 s — PRIYA.**
Medium close-up of PRIYA RAMAN — a 29-year-old Indian-American woman, severe centre-parted black bob, round gold-rimmed glasses, grey puffer vest over a black turtleneck — seated facing the camera in the kitchen area with its white tiled wall, three brass kombucha taps and glowing neon pink 'SNACKS' sign; over her left shoulder, in soft focus, THE DOG, a golden retriever wearing an inflated orange pumpkin costume with a green stem collar, lies down and turns its muzzle to point directly downwind of Gerald, off-screen. She gazes steadily into the lens, one slow blink before speaking, grey foam-covered interview microphone held still at chest height below her chin, and says: 'Forty-one steps from my desk. The first fifteen are the hardest.' Her voice is warm, unhurried, entirely sincere, landing softly. The tiles glow pink from the neon. Sound: a kombucha tap gurgling, faint fizz, low room tone.

**5. MEDIUM — kitchen waterhole, 8 s — no on-screen speaker (narrator voice-over).**
A static medium shot of the kitchen area with its white tiled wall, three brass kombucha taps and glowing neon pink 'SNACKS' sign: four employees stand in single file waiting with labelled ceramic mugs as KEVIN OSEI — a 38-year-old Ghanaian-British man, black buzz cut, matte black helmet-style headphones worn permanently over his ears, charcoal hoodie — steps past the head of the line and fills his mug at the brass tap while the line waits without protest; THE DOG, a golden retriever wearing an inflated orange pumpkin costume with a green stem collar, lies across the end of the queue like a fallen log. Sound: liquid pouring, a hiss of carbonation, one quiet defeated sigh, and the hushed British narrator: 'Even at the waterhole, the alpha never waits his turn, and no one says anything.'

**7. MEDIUM CLOSE-UP INTERVIEW — entrance area, 8 s — DEREK.**
Medium close-up of DEREK LU — a 26-year-old Chinese-American man, thin sandy-blond hair combed across his scalp, small stud earring in his left ear, wrinkled pale blue button-down shirt — facing the camera in the entrance area with its double-leaf glass door and the large carved jack-o'-lantern grinning on the wooden trestle table against the brick; behind him, drifting slowly out of frame, the empty olive-green fleece vest over a navy blazer hovers upright on its concealed aluminium frame. Medium close-up of MARISOL REYES — a 24-year-old Filipina-American woman, dark brown hair in a high tight ponytail, rust-orange flannel shirt, silver hoop earrings — facing the camera in front of the brick wall dressed with a garland of carved pumpkins and orange tinsel, grey foam-covered interview microphone held still at chest height below her chin. Medium close-up of DEREK LU — a 26-year-old Chinese-American man, thin sandy-blond hair combed across his scalp, small stud earring in his left ear, wrinkled pale blue button-down shirt — facing the camera in the converted-warehouse office of exposed steel beams, raw red brick walls and long oak desks with glowing monitors, orange string lights along the beams; behind him, drifting silently through the soft focus, the empty olive-green fleece vest over a navy blazer hovers past on its concealed aluminium frame. **15. WIDE — open-plan office, night, 8 s — no on-screen speaker (narrator voice-over).**

**16. MEDIUM CLOSE-UP INTERVIEW — open-plan office, night, 8 s — GERALD (final button).**
Medium close-up of GERALD SHAW — a 45-year-old white man, neat silver-grey beard, black cashmere V-neck sweater, silver-framed aviator glasses — facing the camera in the darkened converted-warehouse office of exposed steel beams and raw red brick walls, orange string lights and glowing jack-o'-lanterns behind him; over his right shoulder, in soft focus, the costumed tribe stays huddled before the television showing the ghillie-suited intern, and THE DOG, a golden retriever wearing an inflated orange pumpkin costume with a green stem collar, lifts its head and stares dead into the lens. **Cut rationale (for the edit, not on screen):** every 8-second shot carries exactly one gag and one speaker maximum; the narrator's voice is the only continuous thread, which is what makes its reveal in shot 13 land; shots 12 and 14 rhyme (her nest, then his), and shot 15 hands the documentary gaze to the animals so shot 16 can close the film from the other side of the lens. The dog's stare into camera in shots 4, 15 and 16 is the film's quiet refrain.
