# THE SUNSET CLOWN
### A Halloween week news report, San Francisco

**Premise.** It is Halloween week in San Francisco and "News Nightly" is running a Very Serious package on a clown sighted in the Outer Sunset. Every witness tells the plain truth exactly as they experienced it — aggrieved, hurt, bewildered, weirdly comforted — while forty feet behind them, in the same shot, the clown quietly conducts a second, silent, ever-expanding story: re-raking lawns, toilet-papering a Subaru, sitting on a stranger's bench with two coffees, captaining a Muni bus, eating a piñata, buying three hundred pumpkins in warm quarters, and finally standing at the police podium wearing a lit jack-o'-lantern like a bucket. Nobody ever looks at him; the news cycle grinds on in perfect professional deadpan, and the police insist there is no clown. The escalation ends where the news itself does: the anchor signs off, his chair is found empty but for his folded glasses, and the clown is sitting at the anchor desk, hands on a neat stack of scripts, giving the camera a slow thumbs-up beside a lit jack-o'-lantern, as one long sad rubber horn honks under the closing theme. Nobody in San Francisco ever turns around, and the film never winks.

---

## 2. Shape

- **Aspect:** 9:16 portrait, HD.
- **Length:** about 2 minutes 48 seconds.
- **Shots:** 22 single continuous shots — eighteen at 8 seconds (a line of 12–16 words), four at 6 seconds (a line of 11–12 words, or silent).
- **Spine:** anchor intro → field reporter stand-up → witness chorus (car owner, tech worker, fisherman, bus driver, mother, pumpkin seller, bodega owner, costumed father) → the clown's own street → the police denial → night hand-back from the Golden Gate Bridge → studio update → sign-off → button.

**Filming rules honoured throughout:** one visible speaker per speaking shot, in a medium close-up or close-up, facing camera or three-quarter; interview microphone held at the chest, below the chin; no smile or grin while speaking; the head does not lean, walk or look down during the line; the spoken line fills the shot at roughly two words per second; anyone else in frame is silent. The clown never speaks, never enters the foreground of a speaking shot, and is never looked at by anyone.

## 5. Continuity notes for the renderer

- The Clown's description is word-for-word identical in every shot he appears in; keep it verbatim so the same face, nose and jumpsuit recur.
- The Clown is always silent, always at least thirty inches behind any speaker and usually forty feet away, and never addressed, looked at, or lit as the subject — except in shot 22, where he is the subject for the first time.
- No on-screen text is legible or load-bearing anywhere: the anchor's desk cube, the police seal in shot 16, the pickup's price boards in shot 12, the bus destination sign in shot 9 and the police tape in shot 14 should all read as featureless shapes.
- Every Halloween element is played as ordinary set dressing in a city that has simply never taken the decorations down: paper bats, jack-o'-lanterns, cobweb cotton, a witch's cape, inflatable pumpkins, one block party. The only human in costume is Tommy Sandoval in shot 17.
- All dialogue is delivered at documentary sincerity. The comedy lives entirely in the gap between the foreground grievance and the background activity, and in the fact that not one person in San Francisco ever turns around.
