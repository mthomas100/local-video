# The Valley of Funding

A hushed, reverent BBC-style nature documentary films one week of a San Francisco tech startup on Valencia Street during Halloween week, treating the employees as wildlife: the badge-tap territory marks, the great 3 p.m. migration to the snack kitchen, the alpha engineer and his rival, and the costume contest as mating ritual — now judged by an apex predator from Sand Hill Road. In cutaway interviews every employee is gravely, absurdly serious about their habitat. The conceit that holds: a silent office clown stands in the background of every single shot and no character ever notices him. The twist: the whispered narrator's voice belongs to Colin, the night-shift janitor, revealed at last in the supply closet — and the film ends on the one creature no one has ever noticed, alone at 2 a.m., turning his head to the lens.

## Shape
Vertical 9:16, 720x1280 (HD). Fifteen shots, all 8 s = 120 s. [quality] on shots 1, 14 and 15. Narrator voice-over (off-screen) on shots 1, 2, 4, 10, 13, 15; on-camera speech in shots 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 11, 12, 14.

## Cast
- `narrator` — the whispered documentary voice, revealed only in shot 14 as the night janitor: Colin, 60s, British, lean and straight-backed, white hair cropped short, grey janitor uniform over a pressed shirt, headset with a slim boom mic — never seen on camera before shot 14
- `clown` — an employee no character ever notices, and who never speaks: about 40, lanky, white face paint with blue diamonds around the eyes, rainbow afro wig, oversized polka-dot jumpsuit, enormous shoes
- `priya` — Head of Growth: 30s, sharp blunt black bob, mustard blazer over white tee, tablet always in hand
- `sofia` — designer, the forager: late 20s, shaved-side undercut dyed orange, paint-splattered overalls, a labeled cold-brew cup held like a specimen
- `marcus` — the alpha staff engineer: 40s, grey goatee, black hoodie, noise-cancelling headphones around his neck, slow to blink
- `todd` — the rival staff engineer: early 30s, salmon polo tucked into chinos, a smart ring on every finger, blindingly white teeth
- `gwen` — the founder-CEO: 40s, cashmere vest over a band tee, pearl necklace, moves fast, smiles constantly
- `elena` — the predator, a Sand Hill venture partner: 50s, tall, black column coat, silver bob, hands folded, almost no movement
- `colin` — see `narrator`; in shots 2, 6, 9 and 13 he is only background: a lean older man in a grey uniform mopping, glancing at the camera one beat too long

## Sound
Constant low office ambience — HVAC hum, distant keyboard clatter, a Muni rumble through the floor — under the narrator's whisper; party reverb and crowd swell in the contest shots; after dark, hum and a lone siren.

## Shots

1. **Establishing aerial-to-twin-shot, Valencia Street at dawn, 8 s, narrator.** [offscreen] Dawn fog pours over the Mission's paint-red Victorians down Valencia Street, jack-o'-lanterns grinning on every stoop with bat cutouts in the gabled windows; the camera drifts toward the top floor of a glass office box above a laundromat, where cardboard skeletons are taped to the panes and one window shows a motionless clown silhouette — white face paint, rainbow wig, polka-dot jumpsuit — before the lights flick on; far off, the Transamerica Pyramid's lit spire floats above the fog like a shark fin. The narrator's breath hovers close to the microphone, and he whispers, hushed and reverent with a smile audible under it: 'The Mission, early October, the last wild valley of the startup, where the herd still gathers, funded, oblivious.' Cold blue dawn light on the fog and the glass. Sound: fog-horn lowing, a crow calling, distant Muni rumble.

2. **Wide interior, open-plan office floor, 9 a.m., 8 s, narrator.** [offscreen] The glass entry door of the open-plan office beams softly as employees file in one by one, each tapping the badge sensor once, then tapping again when it doesn't chime, then sighing; paper cobwebs stretch between two empty Aeron chairs where a grinning plastic skeleton sits swiveled to a dead monitor; past the window behind the queue, the clown — white face paint, rainbow wig, polka-dot jumpsuit — stands motionless at the coat rack, his coat already hung; at the near edge of frame a lean older caretaker in a grey uniform mops a slow figure-eight and glances toward the camera one beat too long. The narrator draws a soft breath before the mic, barely above a murmur, and whispers, tender as a grandfather: 'Each morning the tribe marks its territory: a badge tap, and one sigh audible from the coffee machine.' Grey morning light through the cobwebbed windows. Sound: door chime repeating, sighing, mop swishing, keyboard clatter warming up.

3. **Interview medium close-up, whiteboard wall, 8 s, Priya.** Against a whiteboard of a red downward-trending line draped in a paper-cobweb garland, Priya, 30s, sharp blunt black bob, mustard blazer over a white tee, fills the frame from chest up and addresses the lens directly; behind her, out of focus, the clown — white face paint, rainbow wig, polka-dot jumpsuit — stands at the copier utterly still while a single helium balloon tied to his wrist rises slowly toward the ceiling. Priya lifts her chin, keeps her eyes locked on the lens, and says, clipped and bright, landing the last word like a closing bell: 'We're not a family. Families disappoint you. We're a growth curve with snacks.' The copier's flash strobes the wall behind the clown's unmoving figure. Sound: copier whir, balloon squeak, distant espresso machine hiss.

4. **Tracking wide, corridor to snack kitchen, 3 p.m., 8 s, narrator.** [offscreen] In a corridor streamer-draped with orange cobweb garland, two dozen employees rise from standing desks in perfect unison and stream at a brisk walk toward the snack kitchen doorway, jostling gently at the pinch; the clown — white face paint, rainbow wig, polka-dot jumpsuit — stands planted mid-corridor like a rock in a river, motionless behind the smoothly parting flow of bodies; inside the kitchen doorway a carved pumpkin glows on the counter. The narrator's breath quickens faintly with excitement, and he whispers, faster now, delighted: 'At three o'clock, the scent of a restocked cabinet triggers the great migration: forty seconds, one broken elevator.' Warm afternoon light stripes the moving crowd through the west windows. Sound: shuffling footfall swell, a dead elevator's hollow ding, one distant triumphant war cry over the last oat milk.

5. **Interview medium shot, snack kitchen, 8 s, Sofia.** Beside the kombucha tap with a glowing jack-o'-lantern on the counter, Sofia, late 20s, shaved-side undercut dyed orange, paint-splattered overalls, holds up a cold-brew cup with a name label like courtroom evidence, framed from waist up; behind her, out of focus, the clown — white face paint, rainbow wig, polka-dot jumpsuit — fills a tiny watering can from the tap until it overflows, then stands motionless. Sofia's eyes narrow to slits, her jaw setting, and she says, low and patient as a threat, savoring each syllable: 'I forage alone. And whoever sipped my labeled cold brew — I know your calendar color.' The pumpkin's candle flickers in the tap's drip. Sound: kombucha fizz, tap drip, fridge hum.

6. **Interview, low-lit engineering corner, 8 s, Marcus.** In the dim engineering corner, three monitors glowing green with uptime graphs and a cobweb garland strung across them, Marcus, 40s, grey goatee, black hoodie, noise-cancelling headphones around his neck, is framed from the chest up in his chair, a sleeping bag visible under the desk; behind him, out of focus, the caretaker in the grey uniform, Colin, empties the wastebasket without looking at Marcus, and past the window the clown's rainbow wig passes the glass on a phone call no one can hear. Marcus blinks once, slowly, his eyes never leaving the middle monitor, and says, a slow bass rumble, one word laid on the next: 'Nine hundred days on call. My heart rate and my uptime are the same number, and neither drops.' Green monitor glow fills the corner and the fog beyond the window. Sound: server fan hum, wastebasket clatter, single keystroke.

7. **Two-shot, facing desks, 8 s, Marcus and Todd.** In a two-shot between two desks bolted face to face, Marcus, 40s, grey goatee, black hoodie, and Todd, early 30s, salmon polo tucked into chinos, smart ring on every finger, hammer mechanical keyboards in duel, framed from the waist up, backs squared to each other over a desk divider from which a plastic Halloween spider dangles on its thread; behind them, out of focus, the clown — white face paint, rainbow wig, polka-dot jumpsuit — applauds both of them, one slow clap at a time, unnoticed. Marcus's jaw tightens, eyes on his keys, and he mutters, gravelly, never breaking rhythm: 'This keyboard is the only thing in this building I tuned.' Then Todd's white teeth flash in a grin, his head tipping back, and he answers, loud and crowing over the clatter: 'Cute. I deployed to production from a kayak this morning.' Overhead strip light glints off both keyboards. Sound: dual mechanical-keyboard fusillade, single slow clown clap.

8. **Interview medium close-up, window with Bay Bridge, 8 s, Todd.** Before a tall window where the Bay Bridge's lights arc over dark water, Todd, early 30s, salmon polo tucked into chinos, a smart ring on every finger, blindingly white teeth, fills the frame from the chest up, holographic bat decorations taped to the glass beside him; behind him, out of focus on the far side of the floor, the clown — white face paint, rainbow wig, polka-dot jumpsuit — tries to sit on an exercise ball that is clearly too small for him. Todd tips his chin up, flashing his teeth at the lens, and says, loud and fast, brimming with confidence three sizes too big: 'Marcus calls my stack a liability. I call it a lifestyle. My therapist calls it Tuesday.' Cold blue city light from the bridge behind him. Sound: traffic hum through glass, squeaking exercise ball, HVAC.

9. **Interview walk-and-talk, pumpkin-lined hallway, 8 s, Gwen.** Along a hallway walled with carved jack-o'-lanterns on pedestals, Gwen, 40s, cashmere vest over a band tee, pearl necklace, walks briskly beside the camera, framed from the waist up, gesturing with both hands; behind her, trailing through the pumpkins, the clown — white face paint, rainbow wig, polka-dot jumpsuit — places a single fun-size candy bar into the outstretched hand of a cardboard cutout of a founder, and the caretaker in the grey uniform leans on his mop at the far end of the hall, watching the lens. Gwen's smile stays fixed while her eyes go flat and calculating, and she says, chirpy and rapid-fire, the last word dropped like a lead weight: 'A Sand Hill judge is watching this year's contest. Participation used to be optional. It isn't.' Warm pumpkin candlelight and cold corridor strips alternate down the hall. Sound: quick footsteps, candy wrappers rustling, mop bucket wheel squeak.

10. **Wide, costume contest floor, night, 8 s, narrator.** [offscreen] The communal floor has become a contest runway under a fog machine's low cloud: a witch in a smartwatch and laptop satchel, a pirate in a venture-capital vest, an employee dressed as a blockchain of literal boxes, dancers flanking a streamer-draped catwalk as the crowd whoops; at the far edge of the room behind the dancers, a tall woman in a black column coat, silver bob, hands folded — Elena — stands utterly unmoved while the crowd flows around her, and beside her the clown — white face paint, rainbow wig, polka-dot jumpsuit — holds a punch ladle over an empty bowl, motionless. The narrator's breath catches with delight, and he whispers, grand and giddy: 'The costume contest: the great mating dance of the Bay. Plumage is Series A. Confidence is entirely borrowed.' Colored party light sweeps the fog and the crowd. Sound: party bass thump, whoops, fog machine hiss, ladle clink.

11. **Interview medium close-up, contest floor corner, 8 s, Elena.** Seated in the plastic skeleton's Aeron chair at the noisy contest floor's edge, Elena, 50s, tall, black column coat, silver bob, hands folded in her lap, is framed from the chest up, the party a blur behind her; behind and to one side, out of focus, the clown — white face paint, rainbow wig, polka-dot jumpsuit — ladles punch into the still-empty bowl, never spilling a drop, never moving otherwise. Elena's eyes hold the lens without blinking, her hands staying folded, and she says, soft and unhurried, each word placed like furniture: 'I back founders who don't flinch. Everyone here is flinching at me right now. I find that coachable.' The fog machine's cloud rolls past her shoulders; party light strobes softly behind her stillness. Sound: thumping bass muffled, one ladle clink, crowd roar distant.

12. **Medium shot, contest stage, 8 s, Gwen.** On a low stage under orange paper garlands, Gwen, 40s, cashmere vest over a band tee, pearl necklace, holds a microphone at chest height, framed from the waist up, a golden trophy on a pedestal beside her; behind her, out of focus, Todd poses alone in his salmon polo, no costume, arms flexed in victory, while the clown — white face paint, rainbow wig, polka-dot jumpsuit — stands at the trophy table replacing a second, spare trophy onto the shelf, and the crowd beyond cheers. Gwen beams, her eyes sweeping the roaring crowd, and she says into the mic, gleeful, milking the pause for all it holds: 'And the best costume goes to Todd — for the sheer audacity of showing up as himself.' Stage light flares gold on the garlands and the trophy. Sound: crowd roar and wolf-whistles, mic feedback squeal, one slow clown clap.

13. **Wide, contest floor winding down, 8 s, narrator.** [offscreen] The party exhales: employees toast boxed wine under the cobweb garlands as Elena, in her black column coat, shakes Gwen's hand at the door; with two fingers she drops a folded term sheet into the plastic candy cauldron, on top of the fun-size bars, then walks out into the night without turning; behind the toasting crowd, the caretaker in the grey uniform pauses mid-mop and looks directly into the camera one full beat too long, and beside the coat rack the clown — white face paint, rainbow wig, polka-dot jumpsuit — hangs his own coat next to hers, as if he lives here. The narrator lets out a long, satisfied breath, and he whispers, warm with awe: 'The predator leaves her candy in the bowl — a term sheet, folded, beside the fun-size Snickers.' String lights and streetlight through the glass warm the mingled crowd. Sound: relieved laughter, wine-carton pour, door chime, mop bucket settling.

14. **Reveal, medium close-up, supply closet, night, 8 s, Colin.** [quality] Inside the janitor's supply closet under a single bare bulb, shelves of mop buckets and cobweb streamers behind him, Colin, 60s, British, lean and straight-backed, white hair cropped short, grey janitor uniform over a pressed shirt, headset with a slim boom mic at his cheek, turns from the shelf; through the closet's small window behind him, across the dark emptied office floor, the clown sits motionless at his desk, a fading jack-o'-lantern beside him. Colin swivels to the lens, raises one finger to his lips in a slow wink, and says, in the exact hushed, reverent whisper of the whole film, mischief glowing under it: 'Twenty-two months mopping this floor. Nobody learns your name. It's the best job in the world.' The bare bulb swings faintly, moving the shadows on the shelving. Sound: sink drip, mop handle settling, party bass three floors down.

15. **Button, wide slow push-in, office floor at 2 a.m., 8 s, clown and narrator.** [offscreen] [quality] The emptied office at 2 a.m., one desk lamp burning: the clown — white face paint with blue diamonds, rainbow wig, oversized polka-dot jumpsuit, enormous shoes — sits ram-rod still at his own desk in the skeleton's swiveled chair, hands folded on the keyboard, a half-burned jack-o'-lantern grinning beside him, the Transamerica Pyramid's spire glowing through the window behind; the camera creeps toward him through the dark cubicle canyon. The narrator's breath returns to the microphone, soft as a secret over the dark floor, and he whispers, wonder in every syllable: 'The only creature in this habitat that no one has ever noticed, still here. Still at his desk.' Then, as the whisper ends, the clown's head turns, slowly, all the way, to face the lens. Lamplight pools on the desk and the pumpkin's glow dims toward dark. Sound: HVAC hum, a lone siren, one long leather-creak of the chair as the head turns.
