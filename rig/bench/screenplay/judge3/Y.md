# PER MY LAST EMAIL

**Premise.** A hushed, deadly serious Attenborough-style nature documentary films a San Francisco tech startup during Halloween week as though the open-plan office off Mission Street were a habitat and its employees a colony of strange, caffeinated creatures: the migration to the snack kitchen, territory marked in cold-brew cans, mating displays at the costume contest, an intern so unseen he has worn a ghost costume to work for eight months without a single colleague noticing. Everyone plays it utterly straight — the animals never know they are animals, and the narrator never once raises his voice — and the escalation ends on the twist: the mystery winner of the costume contest, walking to the awards podium in a brown corduroy jacket, is the narrator himself, who confesses to the camera that for eight years he has watched this colony from a glass booth, and that of everyone in the room he is the only one in costume — he has been dressing as a nature documentarian every single day.

## Shape

- **Aspect:** Vertical 9:16, HD (1080x1920)
- **Length:** 2 minutes 6 seconds
- **Shots:** 16 continuous shots — fifteen at 8 seconds, one at 6 seconds

## The Shots

**1. Wide establishing shot — Mission Street exterior at dusk — 8s — no one on camera, Narrator (V.O.)**
A wet San Francisco street at dusk, cable-car tracks gleaming in the asphalt, the camera at sidewalk level in vertical frame. A converted four-storey red-brick warehouse fills the frame, its ground floor a single wall of black-framed glass glowing warm; the pavement outside is crowded with tall stacks of orange pumpkins, a lopsided inflatable black cat, and strings of tiny orange fairy lights hung from the loading bay, and thick grey fog rolls in off the street swallowing the lower half of the distant Salesforce Tower, a tapering glass skyscraper lit near its crown. The camera drifts in very slowly toward the glass. The Narrator whispers, breathy and reverent, in a line of about twenty words: 'This is the habitat of the San Francisco tech startup — and tonight, the colony begins to decorate.' Sound: a distant foghorn, damp traffic hiss, fairy lights buzzing faintly, and a single low cello note under the narrator's whisper.

**2. Medium close-up — open office, Derek at his standing desk — 8s — Derek speaking**
Inside the open-plan office: long oak desks on casters, rows of monitors, hanging pothos plants, fake cobwebs stretched between two lamp posts. Derek, the alpha — man in his late thirties, short salt-and-pepper beard, red flannel shirt over a grey t-shirt, wire-rimmed glasses — fills the vertical frame from the chest, head and shoulders, facing three-quarter to camera, a flesh-coloured interview microphone held at chest height well below his chin. He lowers his gaze straight down the lens, blinks once, holds his head perfectly level, and says, 'The pager went at three a.m. I fixed everything before sunrise. That is what an alpha is.' His voice is low, measured, no boast in it, landing hard on 'is'. Behind him, out of focus, a coworker in a full black vampire cape methodically waters a desk fern with a green watering can, and a second engineer in a cardboard robot helmet types without looking up. The light is cool monitor-blue mixed with warm overhead strip light. Sound: a server rack humming, scattered keyboard clicks, the trickle of the watering can, and a soft marimba pulse.

**3. Wide tracking shot — the office snack kitchen, 3 p.m. — 8s — six unnamed employees, Narrator (V.O.)**
The office snack kitchen: a marble-topped island with stools, a tall chrome espresso machine, a brass cold-brew tap on the counter, a large ceramic bowl overflowing with orange pumpkins, fairy lights strung beneath the upper cupboards, a window grey with fog. In vertical frame, six costumed employees shuffle in a slow single file toward the island, moving right to left: a man in a grey hoodie carries an empty stainless steel mug held out in front of him like a hatchling he is protecting; a woman in a black cat-ear headband slides silently along the wall behind the queue without moving her feet; at the front, a man in a full body hot-dog costume stands motionless before the cold-brew tap as it drips, doing nothing. The camera tracks sideways alongside the queue at hip height, then tilts up. The Narrator whispers, hushed with awe, in a line of about eighteen words: 'At fifteen hundred hours, the colony migrates to the kitchen, drawn across the carpet by a rumour of last cold brew.' The light is flat and fluorescent, slightly green, with the pumpkins the only warm colour. Sound: a chorus of sneaker squeaks and sock-shuffles on carpet, the espresso machine's steam hiss, one lonely drip into a metal tray, and a pizzicato string bed under the whisper.

**4. Medium close-up — the snack kitchen, in front of the espresso machine — 8s — Sloane speaking**
The same snack kitchen, marble island and chrome espresso machine behind her, the bowl of pumpkins soft out of focus. Sloane, the product manager — woman in her early thirties, sharp blonde bob, olive-green blazer, small gold hoop earrings — is centred in the vertical frame from the chest up, facing the camera, a flesh-coloured interview microphone held at chest height below her chin. She holds her chin level, lifts one eyebrow exactly a millimetre, and says, 'Our daily stand-up happens at the espresso machine, because it's the only place anyone stands voluntarily.' Her voice is bright, clipped, newsreader-crisp, landing on 'voluntarily' with total satisfaction. Behind her, out of focus, the man in the hot-dog costume fills a protein shaker bottle from the cold-brew tap and the foaming overflow runs unnoticed onto the marble. The light is warm fluorescent with a bright chrome highlight on the machine. Sound: the espresso machine idling and hissing, the tap running, the shaker bottle clattering, and a dry marimba tick on the last word.

**5. Medium two-shot — the snack kitchen island — 8s — two silent rivals, Narrator (V.O.)**
The snack kitchen island, seen low across the marble top. A carved jack-o'-lantern with a sagging carved grin sits between the two subjects like a carcass. On the left, a woman in her forties with a sleek black ponytail, black turtleneck and a wide gold cuff; on the right, a man in his fifties with a silver mustache, a navy quarter-zip sweater and a lanyard around his neck. Both are frozen in profile, both hands closed on the same final can under the cold-brew tap, neither blinking; their knuckles whiten. From below the frame a fourth arm in a white skeleton-print sleeve rises, lifts the can away, and retreats; the two rivals slowly turn their heads to follow it, still not blinking. The Narrator whispers, silky and amused, in a line of about nineteen words: 'The more expensive creature always wins the last cold brew, by typing the words per my last email.' The light is low, warm, hard side-light throwing both faces in relief. Sound: two sharp inhales, the clink of the can being taken, a skeleton-sleeve wristwatch ticking, and a low brass sting under the whisper.

**6. Close detail shot, then tilt — Derek's standing desk — 8s — only Derek's hand, Narrator (V.O.)**
Derek's territory in the open office: a standing desk with three vertical monitors showing only glowing colour fields and no readable text, a chunky mechanical keyboard, a stress ball shaped like a flame, and on the desktop a totem of thirty-one empty aluminium cold-brew cans stacked in a wobbling column; one single grey alpaca sock is folded over the drawer handle. The camera holds on the totem, then tilts up as Derek's hand — man in his late thirties, short salt-and-pepper beard, red flannel shirt over a grey t-shirt, wire-rimmed glasses visible at the top of frame — places a thirty-second can on the column without looking, and withdraws. The Narrator whispers gravely, in a line of about twenty words: 'The alpha marks his territory with empty cold brew cans. Every rival counts them nightly, and none of them ever count fewer.' The desk is lit only by the blue glow of the monitors and the red light of a computer tower fan. Sound: the click of aluminium on aluminium, the fan's hum, a distant open-plan office murmur, and a slow low drum pulse.

**7. Medium close-up — the open office, an empty desk — 8s — Toby speaking**
The open office at four in the foggy afternoon: rows of desks, hanging pothos, a swivel chair nobody sits in, cobwebs strung between two monitor arms. Toby, the intern — lanky nineteen-year-old, oversized white bedsheet ghost costume with two cut-out eye holes, black sneakers — is centred in the vertical frame from the waist up, facing the camera, the two eye holes showing his serious young eyes, a flesh-coloured interview microphone held at chest height below the hem of the sheet. His sheet lifts with a shaky breath, he holds perfectly still, and says, 'I've interned here eight months. Nobody has noticed me yet. I'm treating that as momentum.' His voice is soft, airy, faintly hollow inside the sheet, landing on 'momentum' with genuine optimism. Behind him, two engineers walk a wide, careful arc around the empty swivel chair, studiously not touching it or each other. The light is dim grey, a single shaft of afternoon light falling through the fog onto the empty chair. Sound: a printer cycling far away, one sneaker squeak, the whisper of bedsheet, and a lonely cello note.

**8. Medium close-up — the open office, beside Derek's desk — 8s — Derek speaking, with the goat**
The open office beside the standing desk, three vertical monitors glowing behind. Derek — man in his late thirties, short salt-and-pepper beard, red flannel shirt over a grey t-shirt, wire-rimmed glasses — is framed from the chest up, facing three-quarter to camera, interview microphone at chest height below his chin. Beside his shoulder, the office goat — white dairy goat wearing a blue bandana around its neck — stands on a swivel chair and slowly chews the end of a monitor cable, a frayed thread dangling from its mouth. Derek lowers his eyelids once, keeps his head level, and says, 'The goat is the only honest reviewer here. Everyone else, I read in the tickets.' His voice is a rumbly, unhurried baritone, landing gently on 'tickets'. The goat chews louder; Derek does not react to it. The light is cool ambient office light with one warm strip light above. Sound: the soft rhythmic crunch of the goat chewing cable, HVAC hum, a chair wheel creaking, and a single sustained string note.

**9. Medium close-up — the office bathroom mirror — 6s — the werewolf speaking**
The office bathroom, a narrow room with a long mirror, three round vanity bulbs and fogged glass: the werewolf — man in his twenties, grey fur prosthetic muzzle, tall grey fur ears, clip-on claws — stands facing three-quarter to camera with the mirror behind his shoulder, interview microphone at chest height below his furry chin. He points one claw past the lens at his own reflection, holds his muzzle rigid, and says, 'Be honest, be humble, be approximately thirty percent true.' His voice is a low coaching growl, patient, aimed at himself. Through the open door behind him, Toby, the intern, in his oversized white bedsheet ghost costume, drifts past carrying a blue folder, unnoticed. The light is hard white bulb-light on fur, deep shadow behind. Sound: a dripping tap, the buzz of one vanity bulb, and the low growled rehearsal.

**10. Wide shot — the costume contest, the low stage — 8s — costumed crowd, Narrator (V.O.)**
The contest area at the east end of the office: a low plywood stage draped in orange fabric, string lights overhead, a fake cobweb arch, an inflatable black cat wobbling behind the stage, a crowd of about twenty costumed employees below. On stage, a man in his twenties wearing a Kubernetes helm costume — a wide flat white ship's wheel mounted on his back over a grey hoodie with blue LED strips along the sleeves — performs one very slow full pirouette, arms out. The crowd claps politely at even, metronome intervals; three colleagues hold up scorecards showing nothing legible. The camera holds wide and low, then pushes in almost imperceptibly. The Narrator whispers conspiratorially, in a line of about eighteen words: 'His display is a production cluster. In four years of observing him, it has never once been out of date.' The light is warm stage light on the wheel, cool blue office light behind the crowd. Sound: polite applause spaced like a metronome, the faint electronic whir of the LED costume, and a light comic percussion bed.

**11. Medium shot — the contest floor — 8s — Chloe, costumed crowd, Narrator (V.O.)**
The same contest area, string lights and cobweb arch, the crowd parted in the middle of the frame. Chloe, the pricing page — woman in her early thirties, long straight black hair, long white paper dress printed with columns of small black dots, bright red sash across her chest — walks slowly and perfectly level through the parting crowd, arms at her sides, expression unchanged, the paper dress rustling. Behind her, three costumed men simultaneously straighten their shirts; a fourth lifts a whiteboard eraser off a table and holds it for no reason. The camera dollies backward before her as she advances. The Narrator whispers as if holding his breath, in a line of about seventeen words: 'She is the tier marked most popular. Every male in the room immediately attempts to raise his prices.' The light is warm string-light glow from above with cool blue from the office beyond. Sound: rustling paper, two chairs squeaking, a glass clink, and a rising harp glissando under the whisper.

**12. Tracking shot — the open office aisles — 8s — Toby and the recruiter, Narrator (V.O.)**
The open office at night, rows of desks draped in fake cobwebs, orange fairy lights, monitors glowing unattended. The camera tracks laterally at running height: Toby, the intern — lanky nineteen-year-old, oversized white bedsheet ghost costume with two cut-out eye holes, black sneakers — sprints across frame clutching a blue folder to his chest, sheet flapping, sneakers squeaking; six paces behind, the recruiter — woman in her thirties, long dark ponytail, charcoal blazer, holding a single sheet of paper — runs in a dead sprint, arm extended, mouth open in a silent shout. As they pass a desk, the office goat butts the sheet with its horns and Toby stumbles forward. The Narrator whispers rapidly, in a line of about sixteen words: 'The recruiter marks her prey with a calendar invite that declines itself.' The light is dark and amber, lit only by fairy lights and monitor glow. Sound: rapid sneaker squeaks, flapping paper, a goat bleat, and a quick staccato string chase cue.

**13. Medium close-up — the window wall at night — 8s — Marcus speaking**
The office window wall at night, the glass fogged at the edges, the distant tapering glass skyscraper lit near its crown barely visible through the fog. Marcus, the founder — man in his forties, shaved head, black turtleneck, thin silver chain — is centred in the vertical frame from the chest up, facing the camera, interview microphone held at chest height below his chin. He parts his lips slowly, holds his brow perfectly level, and says, 'Fun is a feature. We ship it like any other feature: Friday, broken, with a memo.' His voice is soft, airy, vaguely meditative, landing on 'memo' as though he has said something profound. Behind him, out of focus, the werewolf and the Kubernetes helm stand motionless, both slowly turning to watch him. The light is a single warm key on his face with cold blue city glow behind. Sound: the hum of window glass, distant muffled traffic, the contest thumping faintly through a wall, and one held synthesizer chord.

**14. Static shot — the glass mezzanine booth — 8s — the narrator unseen, Narrator (V.O.)**
A dark glass-fronted announcement booth on the mezzanine overlooking the open office, a small desk lamp the only light inside, a broadcast microphone on a boom arm, a mixing panel with tiny glowing knobs. Inside sits a man in his seventies, silver hair swept back, small round wire spectacles, brown corduroy jacket, seen only from behind and three-quarter, his face not visible, large black headphones over his silver hair as he adjusts the boom toward himself. Far below through the glass, the contest crowd cheers silently at the low stage. The camera holds absolutely still, then inches toward the glass. The Narrator whispers, closer than before, almost to himself, in a line of about nineteen words: 'For eight years I have watched this colony from this booth. Tonight, I do a thing I have never done.' The light is a single warm lamp inside the booth, cold blue office light on the glass. Sound: the crackle of a headset channel, a chair creak, muffled applause far below, and the music dropping to one sustained note.

**15. Medium shot — the low stage, the awards — 8s — Marcus speaking, crowd silent**
The contest area, the low plywood stage under orange fabric and string lights, the crowd of costumed employees packed below the frame edge. Marcus, the founder — man in his forties, shaved head, black turtleneck, thin silver chain — stands at the stage edge holding a small wooden award plaque, facing the camera, interview microphone held at chest height below his chin. He glances down at the plaque, lifts his eyes level to the lens, and says, 'This award honours the creature who watches us daily, and has never once said a word.' His voice is warm, hushed with feeling, landing on 'a word' like a toast. Behind him, the office goat climbs onto the plywood stage and puts its mouth on the ribbon of a second plaque; nobody reacts. The light is warm stage wash on Marcus, cool blue behind. Sound: a feedback squeal from a stage monitor, the crowd holding its breath, plywood creaking under goat hooves.

**16. Medium close-up — the podium, the reveal — 8s — the narrator speaking**
The same low stage, the podium wrapped in orange fabric under string lights, the crowd of costumed employees blurred behind. The narrator — man in his seventies, silver hair swept back, small round wire spectacles, brown corduroy jacket — stands at the podium facing the camera, black headphones hanging round his neck, interview microphone held at chest height below his chin. He glances left, then right, checks no one is listening, keeps his head level, and says in a lowered, delighted, gravelly whisper, 'They believe they dress up for Halloween. They are wrong. I do.' As he speaks he unzips the jacket two inches to reveal a second, identical brown corduroy jacket underneath with a broadcast microphone clipped to it; behind him the goat lifts the wooden award plaque in its mouth and walks off stage with it. The light is warm stage wash on his face, the crowd in cool blue bokeh. Sound: a single distant applause clap, the zip of the jacket, goat hooves and a fluttering paper ribbon fading out, and a final low cello note.
