# The Apes of South of Market
A hushed British nature-documentary narrator films a San Francisco tech startup during Halloween week as though the
office were a wildlife warren: nests of monitors, the great migration to the snack kitchen, the alpha engineer, the
courtship display of the costume contest. The employees give dead-serious to-camera interviews that top the
narrator's framing at every turn, and a lean grey raccoon sits unnoticed in the background of every single shot.
Each October the grid fails; during this year's blackout, alone in the darkened warren, the narrator finally admits
he is speaking of his own kind — he is the raccoon in the ceiling — and the film ends on him on the vent above the
snack kitchen, the stolen gourd trophy in his paws, while nobody, ever, looks up.

## Shape
Vertical 9:16, 1080x1920 HD. Fifteen shots, all 8 s = 120 s. Shots 1, 12 and 15 are [still] establishing/crisis/button
beats and also get [quality]. All interview shots (3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13) are vertical medium close-ups, waist up,
speaker frontal, one speaker per shot, one line per shot at 12-18 words. Every shot carries a Halloween dressing
and the unnoticed raccoon.

## Cast
- `narrator` — the voice: unseen until the final shot, a reverent BBC whisper, hushed at everything, hungry only for candy
- `raccoon` — the unnoticed predator: a lean grey raccoon, black mask across the eyes, long ringed tail, always at the frame edge, mouth always closed, never noticed by anyone
- `devin` — junior backend engineer: 26, lanky, freckled face, curly black hair, frayed grey hoodie
- `priya` — growth lead: 33, sharp bob of straight black hair, red blazer over a band tee, enamel pumpkin pin
- `sven` — staff engineer, the alpha: 44, huge red beard, black wool vest over a grey tee, arms crossed
- `jules` — the intern: 21, slight, round clear glasses, wearing an enormous white cloud costume trailing grey ethernet cable
- `ingrid` — founder and CEO: 47, silver buzzcut, black turtleneck, gold rimless glasses

## Sound
Low HVAC hum and hard keyboard clatter, distant Muni bells and city haze through the glass, paper decorations
rustling, and above it all the close, breathy whisper of the narrator at his microphone.

## Shots
1. **[still][quality] establishing, glass-walled startup on Folsom Street in South of Market, dawn, 8 s, the narrator.**
   The lobby of the warren in the blue fog-light before the herd: a long live-wood reception desk stacked with lit
   jack-o'-lanterns and a bowl of mini candy bars, orange bunting sagging from the ceiling, and through the floor-to-
   ceiling glass the fog-wrapped spike of the Transamerica Pyramid. Perched on the reception desk beside the candy
   bowl sits the raccoon — a lean grey raccoon, black mask across the eyes, long ringed tail curled over the desk edge —
   washing a candy wrapper in both front paws, mouth closed, utterly unnoticed. The narrator takes a long, slow
   breath behind the microphone and whispers, reedy and reverent, barely above a breath: 'South of Market, eight in
   the morning. The warren wakes. Within the hour, thirty-seven apes will log on.' Fog-light through the glass and
   pumpkin candlelight warm in the lobby. Sound: HVAC hum, a distant Muni bell, the crackle of a candy wrapper.

2. **high-angle slow push-in, open-plan bullpen, 8 s, the narrator.** Rows of desks under sagging paper cobwebs:
   three developers, each a silent figure with mouth closed, settle at nests of three monitors apiece ringed with
   empty energy-drink cans, tiny desk skulls grinning beside webcams; one coos softly at his keyboard without
   opening his mouth. Behind them on the windowsill, framed in fog, sits the raccoon — lean grey fur, black eye mask,
   ringed tail — grooming a paw, mouth closed. Monitor-glow fills the bullpen blue and the paper cobwebs sway in the
   air conditioning. The narrator exhales, a long tender breath fogs the microphone, and he murmurs, hushed and soft,
   the way a man speaks in a bird hide: 'The developers build their nests of monitors, coo softly at their keyboards,
   and defend a single stapler.' Sound: keyboard clatter, chair casters, HVAC hum.

3. **interview, vertical medium close-up waist up, desk row with a ghost garland, 8 s, Devin.** Devin, a lanky
   26-year-old with a freckled face, curly black hair and a frayed grey hoodie, sits square and frontal to the lens,
   hands folded. Devin's eyes flick just off the lens, he takes a breath and says, earnest, deadpan, a small crack
   of pride: 'The snack cupboard closes at five. I restock it at four fifty. That is called leadership.' Behind him,
   on the windowsill among paper ghosts, the raccoon — lean grey fur, black eye mask, ringed tail — turns a stolen
   candy bar over and over in its paws, mouth closed, unnoticed. Flat office light fills the walls and the garland.
   Sound: keyboard clatter down the row, HVAC hum.

4. **tracking shot gliding backward down a corridor, 8 s, the narrator.** The great migration: a dozen employees in
   hoodies and vests walk the corridor toward camera in single file, all silent with mouths closed, faces set in
   focus, following a smell; skeleton cutouts and black-orange streamers line the walls; at the very tail of the herd
   trots the raccoon — lean grey fur, black eye mask, long ringed tail — low along the baseboard, mouth closed. The
   herd pauses at the kitchen door; behind the microphone the narrator holds a long breath and whispers, rising with
   awe, a catch of hunger underneath: 'At two PM, a scent moves through the halls. The great migration to the snack
   kitchen begins. Magnificent.' Squirrel-cage bulbs glow along the corridor ceiling. Sound: shuffling feet, streamers
   rustling, one stomach growling.

5. **interview, vertical medium close-up waist up, the snack kitchen, 8 s, Priya.** Priya, a sharp-faced 33-year-
   old with a straight black bob, a red blazer over a band tee and an enamel pumpkin pin, stands frontal to the lens
   in the kitchen doorway. Priya's chin lifts, her eyes slide to the carved-pumpkin gourd bowl on the shelf behind
   her and back, and she says, crisp and rapid, like reading a quarterly number: 'The gourd bowl is the real
   dashboard. It is empty by two PM. That number is called churn.' Behind her on that shelf crouches the raccoon —
   lean grey fur, black eye mask, ringed tail — washing a granola bar, mouth closed, unnoticed. Grey afternoon light
   fills the kitchen. Sound: fridge hum, a blender winding down, wrapper crackle.

6. **full-length slow tilt-up, the alpha's desk bay, 8 s, the narrator.** The alpha at his standing desk: Sven, a
   huge 44-year-old with a red beard, a black wool vest over a grey tee and arms crossed, stares at three monitors,
   silent, mouth closed, while two junior developers, mouths shut, walk his delivered coffee to his desk and back
   away without speaking; a cobweb-draped monitor and a plastic bat hang at his elbow. Behind Sven, on the cable
   tray above the desks, lies the raccoon — lean grey fur, black eye mask, ringed tail draped down — tail flicking,
   mouth closed. The narrator lets out a slow breath and says, low and rueful, a sigh dragging through the words:
   'Sven is the alpha. He does not attend the morning standup. The standup is moved to Sven.' Sound: one heavy
   keyboard, the floor falling quiet.

7. **interview, vertical medium close-up waist up, against orange bunting and a blow-mould skeleton, 8 s, Sven.**
   Sven, 44, red beard, black wool vest, arms still crossed, faces the lens like a man addressing a rival herd.
   Sven's jaw stays still, his brows lift a single millimetre and he says, flat and immovable, each word a nail:
   'The site fell at four. One line fixed it. Nobody thanked me. That is called senior.' Over his shoulder, on a
   shelf behind the skeleton, sits the raccoon — lean grey fur, black eye mask, ringed tail — holding one of the
   skeleton's plastic ribs, mouth closed. Warm bounce light fills the corner. Sound: HVAC hum, distant whoops from
   the contest room.

8. **[quality] slow crane-down, the conference room made lek, evening, 8 s, the narrator.** The contest arena being
   dressed: staff on step stools string orange lights and hang foil bats over a banner of black crepe, all of them
   silent, mouths closed; tables groan under glue guns, feather boas and spools of grey ethernet cable; out the
   window the Mission rooftops go purple at dusk. On the doorframe above the crowd perches the raccoon — lean grey
   fur, black eye mask, ringed tail — batting at a hanging streamer, mouth closed. The narrator's breath quickens
   against the microphone and he whispers, giddy now, the hush trembling: 'Tonight, the conference room becomes the
   display ground. Foam, sequins, one very committed ethernet cable. The courtship begins.' Sound: staple guns, tissue paper
   tearing, a tape measure snapping back.

9. **interview, vertical medium close-up waist up, outside the contest room door, 8 s, Jules.** Jules, a slight
   21-year-old with round clear glasses, wears an enormous white cloud costume, a tail of grey ethernet cable
   coiled on the floor beside him, and faces the lens with total solemnity. Jules's eyes go wide behind his
   glasses, he draws a breath and says, soft, sincere, almost religious: 'They say the cloud is invisible. I
   disagree. Ninety feet of cable. I have never felt more seen.' Behind him the trailing cable twitches by itself:
   the raccoon — lean grey fur, black eye mask, ringed tail — sits at the end of it in the doorway shadow, worrying a
   connector with both paws, mouth closed, unnoticed. String lights glow along the wall behind. Sound: muffled
   applause through the door, cable couplings clicking.

10. **slow lateral dolly past the judges' table, contest room, 8 s, the narrator.** Three judges in foam antennae
    and a paper witch hat sit behind a long table lit by pumpkin candles, all silent, mouths closed, scoring cards
    face-down; before them a line of costumed contenders — a foam rocket, a sequined beaver, the great white cloud
    — all standing proud, mouths closed. On the high shelf above the table, beside the carved-pumpkin trophy, sits
    the raccoon — lean grey fur, black eye mask, ringed tail — gazing down at the trophy, mouth closed. The narrator
    takes a soft breath and whispers, reverent, nearly tender: 'The panel judges the plumage. The contenders present
    their costumes, their poses, their W2s. The gourd trophy gleams.' Sound: candle wax ticking, a glue gun
    dripping, held silence.

11. **interview, vertical medium close-up waist up, the corner office, night, 8 s, Ingrid.** Ingrid, a
    47-year-old with a silver buzzcut, a black turtleneck and gold rimless glasses, sits frontal and utterly calm.
    Ingrid's eyes do not blink, her shoulders stay level and she says, warm, flat, utterly sincere: 'Investors ask
    why nobody leaves. I show them the gourd bowl. Cheap candy. Golden handcuffs.' Behind her, the Bay Bridge lights in the
    glass and a lit jack-o'-lantern on the credenza; on the windowsill behind her shoulder sits the raccoon — lean
    grey fur, black eye mask, ringed tail — turning a laminated badge over in its paws, mouth closed. Desk-lamp
    light fills the office warm. Sound: the city through glass, a far siren, candy rattling in the bowl.

12. **[still][quality] wide, the contest room at its brightest, 8 s, the narrator.** The instant before the failure:
    every light, string light and projector in the room blazing at full, the crowd of costumes mid-cheer, a blue
    spark popping at the ceiling distribution panel above the dance floor — then the grid fails and the whole floor
    dies to black, leaving only the grinning glow of a dozen jack-o'-lanterns on the tables; the costumed apes
    freeze, mouths closed, then a forest of phone torches rises. On the counter's silhouette against the dark
    window, the raccoon — lean grey fur, black eye mask, ringed tail — watches the panel, mouth closed. The narrator
    swallows, audibly, and whispers, grave, almost grateful: 'Each October the grid falters. Tonight it fails. The
    warren goes dark, and thirty-seven apes panic at once.' Sound: a deep electrical thunk, a collective gasp,
    pumpkin candles guttering.

13. **interview, vertical medium close-up waist up, the bullpen under blackout, 8 s, Devin.** Devin, lanky 26,
    freckled, curly black hair, frayed grey hoodie, sits frontal again, now close in the orange glow of a single
    jack-o'-lantern that fills the small room with candlelight and turning shadows. Devin's eyes are glassy, his
    jaw trembles slightly and he says, hoarse, shaken, holding itself together: 'The cloud is in a building. The
    building has no power. I am now praying to a pumpkin.' Behind him, on the counter between two pumpkins, sits
    the raccoon — lean grey fur, black eye mask, ringed tail — perfectly still between the pumpkins, mouth closed. Sound:
    candle wax, distant panicked Slack pings from a single surviving laptop.

14. **slow dolly-in down the darkened corridor, midnight, 8 s, the narrator.** The warren after the herd has gone:
    moonlit fog washes the corridor, a tipped jack-o'-lantern and its seeds spill across the carpet, the deflated
    cloud costume slumps over a chair; and down the exact centre of the corridor, walking unhurried toward the
    lens, comes the raccoon — lean grey fur, black eye mask, long ringed tail — mouth closed, eyes on camera. The
    narrator's breath goes very close and very soft on the microphone and he whispers, intimate, uncharacteristically
    personal: 'One predator shares this warren: unseen, unfed by equity. Forgive me. I speak of my own kind.' Sound:
    tiny deliberate footsteps on carpet, a refrigerator shuddering on backup power.

15. **[still][quality] button, low-angle close-up, the vent above the snack kitchen, 8 s, the narrator.** The
    raccoon — lean grey fur, black eye mask, long ringed tail — squats on the open ceiling vent above the snack kitchen,
    directly over the lens, the stolen carved-pumpkin trophy clutched in both front paws against a torn candy
    wrapper, mouth closed; he looks into the lens and blinks, slowly. Moonlight through the kitchen glass fills the
    ceiling and the vent. The narrator exhales, long and satisfied, and whispers, dry at last, entirely pleased with
    himself: 'Thirty-seven apes. Eleven months. Not one looked up at the ceiling. Rude.' Sound: a candy wrapper
    tearing in the dark, then nothing.
