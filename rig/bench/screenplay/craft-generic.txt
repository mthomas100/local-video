How to make it genuinely funny (work in this order, in your head or in notes, before writing the shots):
- Find the game of the film: the one comic contrast that repeats and escalates. Every shot plays the game once; no shot explains it.
- Brainstorm at least 25 candidate gags (a speaker, their specific line, what happens around them), then keep the best ~15. The best pairs are where what surrounds the line contradicts, quietly confirms, or one-ups it, so the audience gets a second laugh the speaker can't see.
- Make each speaker a distinct, specific person with a want and a voice, and a line that sounds like a real person on camera, not a joke-teller. Specific beats generic: real local detail.
- Escalate toward the end and finish on a button: a final twist or payoff image the line before it sets up, not a summary.
- Deadpan: everyone plays it completely straight. No winking, no explained puns. The genre's own structure is the spine that makes it cohesive.
